External Energy Flexibility

A smart-grid supply framework for inflexible AI data centers
Ali Nasser · PhD Resident

An AI data center draws the same power day and night. Under EEF it takes 100% of its energy from the grid and none of its solar: the solar is sold and runs flexible loads at noon, and at night those loads step back by as much as the data center uses, leaving it room on the grid.

As reliable as a grid-connected data center, as clean as a green one, and the cheapest of all: its solar is sold, and flexible loads replace the battery.

The problem

Clean on paper

Batteries are costly

Needed per 100 MW to reach 90% displacement: about US$39 M a year.

50 MW

Self-supply is capped

SERA caps on-site renewables at 50 MW; a 100-MW campus needs 477 MWp of solar.

474 GW

Grids are filling up

Large loads queued in Texas by mid-2026, about 90% data centers.

How EEF works

1

The data center stays on the grid

It buys 100% of its energy at the tariff, so its reliability is the grid's.

2

Its solar plant is sold to the grid

Sized to its annual use and sold to SPPC; the data center uses none of it.

3

Flexible loads leave room on the grid, hour by hour

Desalination, cooling, pumping and industry use the solar at noon and less at night, anywhere on the grid.

The system: equipment and how it interacts

In Saudi Arabia: SPPC, Saudi Energy and National Grid SA exist; EEF adds an aggregator licence, SERA settlement rules and an hourly registry.

Why 90%, not 100%

85–99.5%displacement where EEF is the best option
8 MW–1.5 GWsize; smaller sites share one contract, larger = groups of campuses
  1. Highest target the same solar plant reaches: solar = annual use with daily shifting tops out at 91.2%.
  2. First target that frees almost all summer evenings (99.2%): no new night peak.
  3. Costs about the same as lower targets: +US$0.3/MWh from 80% to 90%, while emissions halve.
  4. Above 98% needs 2–3× the solar or weekly shifting; 100% is out of reach (storage loss, dusty days, hour order).
