External Energy Flexibility

A smart-grid supply framework for inflexible AI data centers

Ali NasserPhD Resident · Department of Electrical & Computer Engineering

An AI data center draws the same power day and night. EEF keeps it fully on the grid, sells the output of a solar plant to the grid, and pays existing flexible loads to use that solar at noon and to step back at night by as much as the data center uses.

The problem

Why today's options fall short

40%

Clean on paper

Annual renewable certificates cover only 40% of the data center's use hour by hour. At night it still runs on gas and oil.

1,320 MWh

Batteries are costly

Needed per 100 MW to reach 90% displacement, at about US$39 M a year.

50 MW

Self-supply is capped

SERA caps on-site renewables at 50 MW on the same premises. A 100-MW campus needs about 477 MW of solar.

474 GW

Grids are filling up

Large loads waiting to connect in Texas by mid-2026, about 90% of them data centers.

The idea

How EEF works

1

The data center stays on the grid

It buys 100% of its energy at the regulated tariff, so its reliability is the grid's.

2

Its solar plant is sold to the grid

Sized to the data center's annual use; every MWh goes to the Principal Buyer (SPPC).

3

Flexible loads free the same MWh, hour by hour

Desalination, district cooling, water pumping and industry use more at noon and less at night, anywhere on the national grid.

85–99.5%

Displacement

90% is the recommended target: summer evenings are freed with no extra solar. Above 98% needs two to three times the solar, or loads that shift across a week. 100% is out of reach.

8 MW – 1.5 GW

Data-center size

Below about 8 MW the fixed cost of a contract dominates, so small sites should join one contract. Groups of campuses above 1.5 GW behave the same way.

Implementation

Making it work in Saudi Arabia

Most of the actors exist today. EEF needs one new licence, one set of rules and an hourly registry.

SERA

new rules

Licenses the aggregator and sets the baseline, measurement and settlement rules.

SPPC

exists

The Principal Buyer: buys all of the solar output.

Saudi Energy

exists

Supplies the data center at the tariff, unchanged by EEF.

National Grid SA

exists

Checks the day-ahead schedules against network limits.

Aggregator

new licence

Contracts and schedules the flexible loads, and carries the delivery risk.

Hourly registry

new

Issues hourly clean-energy credits, with no double counting.
